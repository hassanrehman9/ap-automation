How it works

Every step has one owner

n8n runs the steps in a fixed order and calls the AI only where judgment is needed. The AI returns words and choices, never numbers it worked out.

1

Invoice arrives by email

A PDF lands in the AP inbox; n8n picks it up and extracts the text.

n8n
2

Read the invoice

Qwen3 4B copies vendor, number, dates, lines and totals exactly as printed.

AI
3

Code it

Granite 4.2 8B picks the GL account, cost center and purchase type, with a reason and a confidence level.

AI
4

Check every number

Line math in cents, Texas sales tax (8.25%, 80% taxable for SaaS), duplicates, the vendor's usual amount, the $2,500 capitalization limit, prepaid periods.

Code
5

Route and post

Clean invoices post; the rest go to review or hold. Each posting carries the AI's coding and reason in its remarks.

ERPNext
6

Approve the risky items and the payment run

I review drafts, release or keep holds, and approve the payment run from an email. Any change to the AI's coding needs a written reason.

Me
7

Reconcile the bank

Code matches bank lines by amount, date window and payee; the AI only reads unclear descriptions; fees become draft journals.

Code

Post

Every check passes, AI confidence is high, coding matches the vendor's usual accounts, and the total is $5,000 or less.

Review

Over $5,000, a fixed asset, lower AI confidence, or coding that differs from the vendor's usual accounts. Saved as a draft for me.

Hold

Math or tax doesn't tie, unknown vendor, invoice number missing or not on the PDF, or more than 3x the vendor's usual amount.

Reject

Same vendor and invoice number already in the books.

Design choices

Why it is built this way

The AI never does math

In my benchmark, all seven local models I tested failed both math problems. So the AI only reads and classifies, and code recomputes every total, tax and difference.

People review where the risk is

High value, assets, unusual amounts and low confidence go to a person. Routine invoices don't wait in a queue.

Everything is logged

Each AI decision, check result, route, posting and approval is written to an audit log, and ERPNext keeps the history of every human change.

No invoice is lost

If ERPNext refuses a posting, the invoice goes to the exception queue with the reason, never silently dropped.

Data stays private

The models run locally through Ollama, so invoice data never leaves the machine. Nothing is exposed to the internet; I reach it over a private network only.

The model is swappable

Moving from local models to a hosted one such as Claude is one setting in n8n. The controls stay the same.

What went wrong, and what I changed

The controls caught most problems. Review caught the rest.

FoundThe AI read an invoice number with two digits swapped, and misread a line as $187.00 instead of $187.50.

OutcomeCode checks held both. I verified them against the PDFs and entered them by hand, with comments.

FoundOne laptop invoice said "for Support team" on a wrapped line; the AI coded it to Engineering, and it posted.

OutcomeMy results review caught it; I amended the invoice. Next control: compare a team named on the invoice with the AI's cost center.

FoundTwo invoices posting at the same moment collided in ERPNext's numbering, and one run stopped.

OutcomeInvoices now post one at a time with retries, and any refusal is routed to the exception queue.

FoundA test batch went out in reverse order, which would have flagged the wrong invoice as the duplicate.

OutcomeI stopped the run, cleaned up the test postings, and now check the first two items before releasing a batch.
